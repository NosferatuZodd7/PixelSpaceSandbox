| Feature | Status | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Benchmark (low profile, 8 ms per frame budget) | ✅ | Scene and tool in place (`src/sim/benchmark_scenario.gd`, `tools/benchmark.gd`); the native kernel meets the budget (0.9–1.4 ms), the GDScript one does not (70 ms), so the game runs on the native kernel (approved). The whole game (`tools/game_benchmark.gd`, here with software rendering in a 4-core virtual machine, editor build): about 80 frames per second at one day per second; at one year per second with a second star in the asteroid belt (2,300 particles woken) the simulation keeps to its 6 ms and lags, at 50 frames per second. NOT EXECUTED: the minimum PC itself (no such machine here) |
| N-body physics; test particles with sleeping and waking | ✅ | Kernel done and tested in GDScript and C++ (identical results, also on Windows). The game screen runs on it: the untouched preset rides its real orbits; the first Cosmos power switches every body to n-body gravity, and each body then shows the orbit it is on right now. Particles are not yet used by the game (belts and comets) |
| Belts, Oort cloud, comets with tails | ✅ | The asteroid belt (with the Kirkwood gaps), the Kuiper belt and plutinos, the Oort cloud (zoom out to 0.002 px/AU to see it as a haze), and Halley, Encke and Hale-Bopp from their real elements, as sleeping particles drawn as single pixels; comets are named and grow a dithered tail pointing away from the star that lights them most (inside 5 AU, longer the closer) |
| Light and gravity fronts at c; gravity ghosts; speed-of-light world law | ✅ | Every Cosmos power sends a front; pull and starlight change only where it has arrived (also for a body removed or flung before its own news had spread); a removed body pulls on as a faint ghost; fronts are drawn as warm (light) and violet (gravity) rings labelled with what changed ("Star 1 appeared"). The law is in the World laws panel |
| Smart time (follow the wave, slow motion on arrival, jump to next arrival) | ✅ | While news of a change travels, two more time buttons appear: jump to its next arrival (N, then pause) and follow the wave (G: the camera watches from where it started and zooms out with it, time runs so it crosses half the view in 3 s and eases down so each arrival at a star or planet takes 1.5 s; choosing a speed takes over again). Inspect windows say when the news will arrive ("Star 1 appeared — arrives in 6 h 52 min"). Also: the game starts at one day per second; the counter warns when the simulation cannot keep up |
| Ghost trajectories | ✅ | Overlay in the Tools tab: after each change, a copy of the system as it was runs alongside (bodies only); each body's would-be path is drawn dashed in violet next to its real one, and a ring marks where the news reached it and the paths part |
| Collisions, impacts, Roche limit (rings, tidal disruption) | ✅ | Bodies that touch merge (mass and momentum kept, volumes add up; a black hole always wins and its horizon grows; two stars make a brighter one), with the news spreading at c so the far field does not jump; a body passing inside the Roche limit of a heavier one is torn into a ring around it (a moon thrown at the Earth, a star too close to a black hole). Both kernels find these at every step. A collision throws up massless debris (most of it falls back) with a flash and a one-pixel shake; impacts of small bodies are reported |
| Cosmos powers (place, fling, delete, second sun, black hole, rogue planet) | ✅ | Cosmos tab with 11 powers from `data/powers.json`: sun-like star, red dwarf, blue giant, white dwarf, black hole, gas giant, rocky planet, moon, rogue planet, fling, delete. Click: circular orbit around what dominates there (both masses counted); drag: throw; rogue planet: sweeps through and leaves; preview of the path while aiming; right click or Esc puts the power away. The habitable zone is drawn from every star's light (two suns bend it into one shape) |
| Timeline: snapshots, rewind, undo | ✅ | A snapshot every 10 s of play and before every change the player makes (30 kept for now; the quality profile will set it); the marks above the toolbar go back to any of them (and pause there); Ctrl+Z undoes the last change. Going back and running again gives exactly the same future (tested) |
| Overlays, ruler, light pulse | ✅ | Tools tab: orbits, fronts, habitable zone, ghost trajectories, Hill spheres and Roche limits, Lagrange points of the selected body, gravity field (one band per tenfold change) and temperature map, each switched on and off; the ruler (AU, km and light-time; an end on a body follows it); the light pulse (a ring of light from a body, with how long it has travelled). Still to come: photo mode and the cinematic camera (with the polish, milestone 9) |
| Notifications; world laws for the cosmos | ✅ | Notices of collisions, bodies torn into rings and impacts (named comets by name) in the top left; click one to follow the body. World laws panel (Tools tab): speed of light real, 10× or 1,000× slower (marked as fiction) or instant; collisions on or off; gravity ×0.5, ×1, ×2. Star ageing joins with milestone 4; the encyclopedia entry on slower light with milestone 9 |
